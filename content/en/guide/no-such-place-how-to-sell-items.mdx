## Item Categorization: What to Keep, Sell, or Dismantle

Blindly selling everything in your bags can leave you stranded when crafting prerequisites appear later. Learning **no such place how to sell items** responsibly involves classifying inventory into strict priority tiers.

Items in the game fall broadly into four functional brackets:

* **Pure Valuables**: Items with explicit flavor text noting commercial interest and zero crafting utility. These should be liquidated immediately.
* **Component Salvage**: Screws, wires, panels, and chemical compounds. Keep a healthy reserve for repairs; sell only when stacks exceed your immediate storage limits.
* **Consumable Overstock**: Low-grade healing supplies, cheap rations, and redundant filters. Excellent for bartering with field scouts.
* **Equipment & Armaments**: Worn equipment often sells for little compared to the resources retrieved by breaking it down at a workbench.

## Troubleshooting Common Inventory and Trading Issues

Many player reports stem from moments where items appear stuck in inventory or interact buttons remain greyed out. When trying to resolve **no such place how to sell items**, keep an eye out for these frequent restrictions:

* **Item Locking**: Items tagged as favorites or placed in quick-access hotbars are locked against accidental sales. Unbind the item from your quickbar before attempting to trade.
* **Quest and Mission Anchors**: Items tied to ongoing narrative leads or open investigations cannot be sold or dropped. Once the associated task resolves, the tag clears.
* **Merchant Scrip Deficits**: If a vendor does not possess enough currency to pay for your bundle, the transaction button locks. You must reduce your offering or accept goods from the merchant's store to balance the scale.
* **Contraband Flags**: Certain outposts enforce contraband bans. Stolen goods or illegal military tech will cause vendors to refuse trade or alert security.

| Issue Encountered | Root Cause | Immediate Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Item not showing in vendor window** | Item is equipped, favorited, or slotted | Unequip item and clear favorite star from bag |
| **"Insufficient Merchant Funds" prompt** | Sale value exceeds merchant's total wallet | Barter for ammo/supplies or visit a larger broker |
| **Drastically reduced sell price** | Market oversaturation or poor faction rep | Travel to a different regional hub or boost standing |
| **Transaction button unclickable** | Trade balance is negative or inventory overloaded | Match offer value evenly or confirm bag space |
| **Merchant refuses to open dialogue** | Player is carrying flagged contraband | Stash illicit goods outside settlement limits |

By identifying these common trade roadblocks early, you save valuable travel time and avoid lugging heavy bundles across dangerous wilderness sectors.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I sell stolen or confiscated items in No Such Place?
Yes, but legitimate city brokers will generally refuse confiscated or stolen merchandise. To liquidate flagged gear, seek out black market fences, rogue wanderers, or underground salvage kiosks operating outside standard faction law.

### Why is the sell price much lower than the buy price?
Vendors apply a baseline trade margin to all stock. Your buying price includes vendor markup, while your selling price reflects wholesale scrap value. You can narrow this gap by increasing your faction standing and unlocking negotiation perks.

### What should I do if a merchant runs out of currency when learning no such place how to sell items?
When a merchant exhausts their currency pool, you have two options: trade your items for high-utility consumables (like ammunition, repair components, or medicine) to balance the trade ledger, or wait for the merchant's inventory and funds to restock after a regional time cycle.

### Is it better to sell weapons or dismantle them?
In almost all scenarios, dismantling worn or standard-tier weapons at a workbench yields crafting components that possess greater utility or higher combined value than the small amount of coin earned from selling broken equipment directly.
